NFL

Daniels-to-Diggs rally sinks the Giants on Thursday night

Jayden Daniels found Stefon Diggs for a 32-yard go-ahead score with under a minute left as Washington erased a fourth-quarter deficit to beat New York 24-21. The win moves the Commanders to 3-5-1, keeps a faint playoff pulse alive, and gives Washington the tiebreaker over the Giants after the two sides tied last month.

Source: ESPN

Rams lose Puka Nacua to injured reserve after a knee reaggravation

Los Angeles placed star receiver Puka Nacua on IR with PCL/MCL sprains and turf toe suffered last week, ruling him out at least four games against Arizona, Green Bay, Kansas City and San Francisco. With Davante Adams already on IR with a hamstring injury, the Rams signed Brennan Presley and will lean on Tutu Atwell, Konata Mumpfield and Xavier Smith in the receiving corps.

Source: ESPN

A trade-deadline redo gone wrong has Harbaugh asking the league to step in

Denver's deadline trade for Chargers linebacker Daiyan Henley fell apart after the Broncos tried to renegotiate the compensation once Henley had already relocated, prompting Chargers coach Jim Harbaugh to ask the league office to review how the deal collapsed. The episode has drawn criticism as an apparent loophole in how the NFL handles trades that unravel after the deadline has passed.

Source: NBC Sports/Pro Football Talk

CFB

NC State turns to program legend Philip Rivers to rebuild the Wolfpack

NC State is finalizing a deal to make alum Philip Rivers, 44, its next head coach, replacing Dave Doeren, who was fired in October; interim coach Todd Goebbel will finish out the season. The full Board of Trustees is expected to formally approve the contract Friday, capping a fast-moving search for a program trying to reset after a rough fall.

Source: WRAL

Florida State's coaching search loses a top target in Jake Dickert

Wake Forest's Jake Dickert is out of the running for Florida State's opening after signing a long-term extension to stay with the Demon Deacons. New athletic director Jeff Purinton, hired just this week, is still working through a field that includes Duke's Manny Diaz, with Mike Norvell's roughly $50 million buyout shaping how far the Seminoles can stretch for their next hire.

Source: CBS Sports/Chop Chat

NBA

Cade Cunningham's 52-point double-overtime masterpiece outlasts Trae Young's buzzer-beater

Cade Cunningham poured in a career-high 52 points with 11 assists and zero turnovers as Detroit outlasted Washington 144-140 in double overtime. Trae Young forced the extra period with a buzzer-beating three and finished with 37 points, but the Pistons' star had the last word.

Source: NBA.com

Curry's injury confirmed as an oblique tear, with a timeline closer to a month

The Warriors confirmed Stephen Curry's MRI showed an oblique abdominal tear, setting a two-week re-evaluation, though ESPN's Shams Charania reports the real recovery could run closer to a full month. It's the first firm diagnosis since Curry left Wednesday's loss to Houston, ending the uncertainty coach Steve Kerr had voiced right after the game.

Source: NBA.com/Warriors

Marcus Smart suspended three games for sucker-punching Charles Bassey

The NBA suspended Houston's Marcus Smart three games without pay, costing him over $125,000, for punching Golden State center Charles Bassey during Wednesday's win over the Warriors. Bassey said afterward that Smart "has been playing dirty the whole game," and the ban keeps Smart out until a Nov. 18 game against Indiana.

Source: Yahoo Sports/Bleacher Report

NCAAM

UConn's Nik Khamenia headed for foot surgery, out 6-8 weeks

No. 4 UConn announced Thursday that forward Nik Khamenia will have surgery on the foot injury that forced him out of Monday's win over Wagner, with coach Dan Hurley saying he'll be reevaluated in six to eight weeks. Khamenia was the Huskies' third-leading scorer at 10.7 points on 62% shooting, and his absence pushes freshmen Colben Landrew and Egor Amosov into bigger roles for upcoming games against ranked Illinois and Kansas.

Source: ESPN

WNBA

Engelbert says commissioner search has reached in-person interviews

Speaking Thursday after the league's competition-committee meetings, outgoing commissioner Cathy Engelbert said search firm Excel Search & Advisory is now interviewing candidates in person as it works toward naming her successor by her Dec. 31 retirement date. She added that if no hire is made in time, she'll stay on until one is, with NBA deputy commissioner Mark Tatum as the fallback to step in on an interim basis.

Source: Yahoo Sports/AP

Kelsey Mitchell headlines a free-agent class testing a newly expanded cap

Fever guard Kelsey Mitchell, coming off a career-best 24.7 points per game, is now an unrestricted free agent after seven seasons in Indiana and is widely rated the top name in a class that also includes Napheesa Collier. Fever GM Amber Cox has called re-signing her a top priority, but cap-strapped rosters like the Sparks, with only five players under contract for next season, are expected to pursue her hard.

Source: Just Women's Sports

MLB

New Astros GM Randy Flores made his first big call, hiring former Yankees bench coach Brad Ausmus — who spent 10 of his 18 playing seasons in Houston — after Joe Espada's contract wasn't renewed following a wild-card sweep. It's Ausmus's third managerial job after stints with the Angels and Tigers, and reaction among fans and local media has been mixed on whether he's the right fit.

Mets emerge in a crowded field chasing Tarik Skubal

With GM Meetings underway in Las Vegas, reports now put the Mets, Dodgers, Blue Jays and Padres all in on ace Tarik Skubal, who's seeking an eight-to-nine-year deal in the $350-425 million range. Golf

NBC's longtime golf voice Dan Hicks dies at 64

Dan Hicks, NBC Sports' lead golf play-by-play announcer since 2000 and the broadcast partner of analyst Johnny Miller for most of that run, has died after what his family described as an acute illness. His wife, ESPN anchor Hannah Storm, announced the death, and tributes poured in from NBC and the PGA Tour for a voice that called the network's biggest tournaments for more than two decades.

Source: PGA Tour

LIV Golf's relaunch financing collapses, and Jon Rahm cuts ties

BC Partners has pulled its roughly $300 million financing plan for a relaunched "LIV Golf 2.0" after too few players committed, a setback that sources say now points toward the league liquidating entirely following its September bankruptcy filing. Jon Rahm has separately reached a settlement surrendering his claim to the rest of his nine-figure contract, telling the bankruptcy court he found the proposed new-league terms unacceptable.

Source: ESPN

Horse Racing

Cheltenham-winning jockey Tom O'Brien dies at 39 after cancer battle

O'Brien, who won five Grade One races including two at the Cheltenham Festival before retiring from riding in 2023 and becoming a British Horseracing Authority steward, has died following a lengthy illness. BHA chief executive Brant Dunshea said he "meant so much to so many people across our sport," and O'Brien is survived by his wife and two young sons.

Source: Sky Sports

White Abarrio's owners drop their Breeders' Cup lawsuit, point blame at state regulators

The owners who sued over last year's late scratch of White Abarrio from the Breeders' Cup Dirt Mile have dismissed their claims against the Breeders' Cup and Del Mar with prejudice, instead laying responsibility on the California Horse Racing Board. No party admitted fault, and the suit's roughly $10 million damages claim over the horse's stud value is now resolved as far as Breeders' Cup and Del Mar are concerned.

Source: Horse Racing Nation

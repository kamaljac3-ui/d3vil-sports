NFL

Packers add veteran tight end Jonnu Smith as Musgrave's return date stays murky

Green Bay signed 31-year-old Jonnu Smith, a 2024 Pro Bowler, to a one-year deal Thursday, giving the Packers insurance behind Tucker Kraft, who's still working back from ACL surgery. Starter Luke Musgrave remains on the physically-unable-to-perform list with a neck injury from June minicamp that could cost him the first six games. "We're working through it to see if it's something long term or not," GM Brian Gutekunst said of Musgrave's timeline.

Source: NBC Sports

Browns cut 2023 third-rounder Cedric Tillman ahead of the roster deadline

Cleveland waived wide receiver Cedric Tillman on Thursday, ending his three-year run (71 catches, 833 yards, 5 touchdowns) after he fell behind KC Concepcion, Denzel Boston and Isaiah Bond on the depth chart during camp. The Browns immediately backfilled a roster spot by claiming cornerback Mekhi Blackmon off waivers from Indianapolis.

Source: NBC Sports

Kyle McCord throws four touchdowns as Packers rally past Cardinals in preseason finale

With Green Bay's starters on the sideline, backup quarterback Kyle McCord threw for 226 yards and four touchdowns — two of them to Kisean Johnson in the final 3:35 — to erase a late deficit and beat Arizona 42-38 on Friday night. The Cardinals also sat their regulars, making it an audition night for players still fighting for a 53-man roster spot.

Source: AP (via WRAL)

CFB

Texas judge halts SEC's crackdown on athletes who signed pro contracts

A Dallas County judge issued a two-week restraining order Thursday blocking the NCAA, the SEC and commissioner Greg Sankey from keeping roughly 41 athletes — including some with NFL contracts — off 2026-27 rosters, undercutting the very rule the conference had just finalized. The order also reopened the transfer portal for the affected players for one week, with a full hearing set for Sept. 11.

Source: AP (via Northwest Arkansas Democrat-Gazette)

College football's season finally kicks off, headlined by a trip to Dublin

Week 0 arrives with North Carolina and TCU opening the season across the Atlantic in the Aer Lingus College Football Classic in Dublin, a rematch of last year's 48-14 TCU rout. Elsewhere, No. 14 USC hosts San Jose State as Lincoln Riley — 9-0 in season openers — breaks in quarterback Jayden Maiava without top receivers Makai Lemon and Ja'Kobi Lane, while Memphis and UNLV close out the day's action at Allegiant Stadium.

Source: ESPN

NBA

Blazers guard Shaedon Sharpe out roughly six months after knee surgery

Portland announced that 23-year-old Shaedon Sharpe underwent surgery Wednesday for a torn lateral meniscus in his right knee, suffered during an individual offseason workout. The timeline points to a return sometime around the trade deadline, opening up significant additional minutes for Scoot Henderson in the backcourt.

Source: NBA.com

Stephen Curry becomes eligible for a new max extension with the Warriors

Saturday marks the first day the 38-year-old Curry can sign a new deal with Golden State — a two-year extension worth roughly $136.7 million that would carry him through the 2028-29 season, his 20th. Curry is entering the final year of his current contract, and Warriors GM Mike Dunleavy has said publicly the team wants a new deal in place before the season starts.

Source: HoopsHype

NCAAM

Same eligibility ruling opens the door for Texas A&M's Rylan Griffen and others

Thursday's Dallas County restraining order against the NCAA and SEC directly affects men's basketball: Texas A&M guard Rylan Griffen, who'd already won a separate court order for a fifth season, is now cleared to return to the Aggies, and former Florida/Mississippi State/UCF guard Riley Kugel is also among the roughly 41 named plaintiffs. The NCAA is expected to fight the order at a follow-up hearing Sept. 11.

Source: On3

Georgetown's Vince Iwuchukwu wins a court order restoring his final season

A New York judge granted Georgetown center Vince Iwuchukwu a temporary restraining order clearing him to play in 2026-27, after the NCAA twice denied his medical waiver request for a fifth year — he missed most of his 2021-22 freshman season at USC following an in-practice cardiac arrest. The NCAA will contest the ruling at a Sept. 1 hearing. "Yeah, I'm back," Iwuchukwu said.

Source: Georgetown Voice

WNBA

DeWanna Bonner joins the Dream after a buyout from the eliminated Mercury

The two-time champion and six-time All-Star reached a buyout with Phoenix after the Mercury were eliminated from playoff contention, then signed a rest-of-season deal with Atlanta at the prorated max. Las Vegas and New York were also reportedly interested; at 39, Bonner is expected to come off the bench as the Dream push for their fourth straight postseason berth.

Emily Engstler's career night lifts Portland past Atlanta, snapping the Dream's win streak

Carla Leite led all scorers with 26 points and six assists for Portland.

Source: KPTV

A'ja Wilson and Jackie Young power the Aces past Toronto heading into the FIBA break

Wilson scored 27 points on efficient interior shooting and Young added 23 points, eight assists and four three-pointers as Las Vegas beat the Tempo 93-73 on Friday, forcing an 18-2 edge in points off turnovers. The Aces (27-13) head into the FIBA World Cup break riding the win, with Wilson still chasing a fifth MVP award.

Source: Las Vegas Review-Journal

Angel Reese's league-leading 8th technical foul gets wiped off the books

Reese picked up her eighth technical of the season during Friday's loss to Portland — a total that would have triggered an automatic one-game suspension for Sunday's game against Minnesota. Hours later, the WNBA rescinded the call, dropping her back to seven and clearing her to play against the Lynx.

Source: ClutchPoints

NCAAW

Two women's basketball players named in the same eligibility lawsuit

Ole Miss guard Christeen Iwuala and guard Bailey Maupin are both plaintiffs in Thursday's Dallas County restraining order, after signing WNBA contracts this spring — Maupin with the Golden State Valkyries, Iwuala with the Washington Mystics — without ever appearing in a regular-season game. The order means neither can be barred from NCAA competition in 2026-27 on the basis of those contracts, at least while litigation continues.

Source: On3

MLB

Reds rookie Sal Stewart breaks a 76-year-old franchise RBI record

Stewart drove in two more runs, including his 29th home run of the season, in Cincinnati's 10-8 win over the Cubs on Friday, pushing his RBI total to 101 and breaking Jim Greengrass' 1953 rookie record. He's the first MLB rookie to reach 100 RBIs since Pete Alonso in 2019, and he currently leads all of baseball in the category.

Source: JOX FM (AP)

Cal Raleigh's power surge has him hitting home runs in bunches for Seattle

Raleigh hit three home runs against Philadelphia earlier in the week — the first three-homer game of his career — then connected in his very first at-bat the next night, tying the Mariners' franchise record for homers in four straight at-bats. He's carrying a five-game hitting streak with seven home runs this month into Seattle's playoff push.

Source: ClickOnDetroit (AP)

Giancarlo Stanton's season is in jeopardy after a new calf injury

The Yankees moved Stanton to the 60-day injured list after he strained his left calf during a baserunning drill while still rehabbing a right calf strain that had sidelined him since April. Manager Aaron Boone acknowledged the new injury, with roughly 34 games left, could end Stanton's season.

Source: Yahoo Sports

NHL

Cale Makar signs the richest contract in NHL history

Colorado and Makar finalized an eight-year, $163.2 million extension announced Friday, making the reigning Norris Trophy winner the first player ever to top $20 million a year. The timing beats a new CBA provision taking effect Sept. 15 that would have capped him at a seven-year term instead of eight.

Source: Yahoo Sports

Jets respond to Connor Hellebuyck's trade request without offering a timeline

A day after Hellebuyck's agent went public with the goaltender's trade request, GM Kevin Cheveldayoff said Winnipeg will "continue to work through the situation in a thoughtful manner," with no update on a deal. Hellebuyck, who's signed through 2030-31 with a full no-movement clause, says he's willing to work with multiple teams to get a trade done, with Buffalo, New Jersey and Carolina mentioned as possible destinations.

Source: Pro Hockey Rumors

NFL

Packers' Josh Jacobs formally charged with misdemeanor battery and property damage

Brown County prosecutors charged Jacobs on Thursday with two misdemeanors stemming from a May 23 domestic incident at his Hobart, Wisconsin home that led to his arrest at the time. The Packers said only that they're cooperating with the NFL's review; Jacobs' initial court appearance isn't until Nov. 17, leaving the situation hanging over Green Bay's backfield as camp wraps up.

Source: ESPN

Seahawks lock up Leonard Williams with a three-year, $90 million extension

Fresh off a Super Bowl-winning, All-Pro season, the 32-year-old defensive tackle tore up the final year of his old deal for a new contract worth roughly $56 million guaranteed. It keeps Seattle's championship front line largely intact and ties Williams with Vita Vea for fifth-highest-paid at his position.

Source: ESPN

Rams and Dolphins swap Tutu Atwell for Jarquez Hunter

Miami sent receiver Tutu Atwell back to the team that drafted him in exchange for running back Jarquez Hunter, a 2025 fourth-round pick who barely saw the field as a rookie. It reunites Atwell with the only NFL franchise he'd known before a brief Dolphins stint, while Miami bets on Hunter's upside in the backfield.

Source: ESPN

NBA

Heat add center depth, signing Nick Richards

Miami rounded out its roster with a one-year, minimum-level deal for free-agent big man Nick Richards, giving Bam Adebayo another frontcourt option behind Bobby Portis. It's the Heat's latest quiet addition after already landing Klay Thompson earlier this offseason.

Source: HoopsHype

NCAAM

SEC finalizes harsh penalties for rostering players who signed pro contracts

The conference's presidents and athletic directors voted 16-0 to formally bar athletes who signed with, or appeared on a roster for, the NFL, NBA, G League or WNBA without properly withdrawing, then approved real teeth behind it 15-0 (LSU abstaining): a half-season coaching suspension, a fine equal to half a sport's annual operating budget, and loss of conference voting rights. Commissioner Greg Sankey can tack on further sanctions, and while the rule was driven largely by football cases, it applies just as directly to men's basketball rosters.

Source: Larry Brown Sports

Kentucky transfer Mark Mitchell appears eligible under the SEC's new policy after all

The former Missouri forward, who won a court order for a fifth season and transferred to Kentucky, saw his eligibility questioned the moment the SEC's new anti-pro-return rule went into effect. But because Mitchell played NBA Summer League with Denver without ever signing a contract, he doesn't meet the policy's disqualifying criteria — meaning he appears clear to suit up for Mark Pope's Wildcats this season.

Source: On3

WNBA

Aces lose NaLyssa Smith for the rest of the season

Smith, the WNBA's field-goal-percentage leader at 63.8%, suffered a non-contact leg injury in Sunday's win over Toronto and will miss Las Vegas's final five regular-season games plus the playoffs. It's a serious blow to the defending champions' repeat bid as they head into the postseason without one of their most efficient scorers.

Source: ESPN

Georgia Amoore's career-high 25 rallies the Mystics past the Mercury

Amoore scored 16 of her career-high 25 points in the fourth quarter, including a career-best six threes, to erase a 10-point deficit and beat Phoenix 80-73. Kiki Iriafen chipped in 20 points and 12 rebounds for her 19th double-double of the season, as Washington swept the season series against the Mercury.

Source: TSN

Valkyries complete a season sweep of the Liberty with their most lopsided win yet

Golden State throttled New York 79-60, holding the Liberty to their lowest point total of the season behind Cecilia Zandalasini's 20 points and Veronica Burton's season-high 13 assists. It's the third win in three tries over New York this year for the Valkyries — the only team the Liberty haven't beaten in 2026.

Source: CBS Sports

NCAAW

Philadelphia's Women's Big 5 unveils a pod format built around the Palestra's 100th birthday

The city's six Division I programs split into two three-team pods — Drexel, Penn and Saint Joseph's in one, La Salle, Temple and Villanova in the other — for round-robin play in November. Pod finishers meet in a championship tripleheader at the Palestra on Dec. 6, part of the arena's centennial celebration.

NFL

Rams' Puka Nacua suspended three games while already on injured reserve

The NFL suspended Puka Nacua three games for violating the personal-conduct policy, a penalty he'll serve while already out on IR, so it costs him roughly $950,000 in pay rather than extra missed games — he can't be reinstated until Dec. 14. The discipline traces to a disputed New Year's Eve incident in which an accuser alleges an antisemitic remark and a bite; Nacua's attorney denies the remark and calls the bite "horseplay," and a related civil suit is still pending.

Source: NFL.com

Ravens send Marlon Humphrey home and bench him over a podcast comment about Baltimore

Baltimore ruled cornerback Marlon Humphrey inactive for Sunday's game against Carolina and sent him home from the facility after he said on his own podcast that the city is "best at making criminals," then apologized at his locker. Coach Jesse Minter framed the move as cutting down a distraction, though Baltimore's mayor said a verbal apology "is not enough."

Source: ESPN

Texans rout a depleted Colts team 26-3 to open Week 11

Houston throttled Indianapolis 26-3 on Thursday Night Football, with both teams missing multiple starters — the Colts without two offensive tackles, a defensive tackle and a cornerback, the Texans without Marvin Harrison Jr. and Danielle Hunter among others.

Source: NFL.com

CFB

Minnesota fires offensive coordinator Greg Harbaugh Jr. for a third time

P.J. Fleck fired offensive coordinator Greg Harbaugh Jr. after a 42-14 loss to Penn State dropped Minnesota to 5-5, ending Harbaugh's third stint with the program with two games left on the schedule. The Gophers ranked near the bottom of the Big Ten in both scoring and yards per game, and Fleck is now searching for his fifth offensive coordinator.

Source: ESPN

UTEP fires Scotty Walden with two games left

UTEP dismissed head coach Scotty Walden mid-season, closing out a three-year tenure that finished 6-28. The school has opened a national search for his replacement.

Source: KFOX14

Indiana's Curt Cignetti keeps needling the CFP committee over his team's ranking

Cignetti has been openly critical of the College Football Playoff selection committee after unbeaten Indiana slid from No. 4 to No. 9 in the latest rankings before climbing back to No. 8, arguing it's biased toward the SEC. Committee chair Hunter Yurachek says it's a matter of other teams' results reshaping the comparative picture, and Cignetti says the snub only "feeds" his team's motivation.

Source: Sports Illustrated

NBA

Damian Lillard scores 33 in his return to Milwaukee as the Bucks' losing streak reaches 12

Playing in Milwaukee for the first time since the Bucks waived him last year, Damian Lillard scored a season-high 33 to lead Portland past Milwaukee 124-114, extending the Bucks' losing streak to 12 games. The Blazers closed the game on a 9-0 run after Tyler Herro had cut the deficit to one, with Milwaukee missing Kyle Kuzma and Myles Turner.

Source: TSN

Victor Wembanyama reveals a dunking-induced blister is changing his game

Wembanyama scored 32 points with 11 rebounds in San Antonio's 124-112 win over the Clippers, but revealed afterward that a blister from dunking so often over his first 16 games has him settling for jumpers instead of attacking the rim, with tape visibly wrapped around his shooting hand. Stephon Castle added 21 points and 12 assists in the win.

Source: ESPN

Karl-Anthony Towns gets the better of his former team as the Knicks edge Minnesota

Towns had 22 points and 12 rebounds against the Timberwolves, the team that traded him away, and Jalen Brunson added 21 points and seven assists as New York held off Minnesota 102-99.

Source: ESPN

Mercy Miller scored 21 points and Houston held off a late Kansas rally to win the Players Era 8 championship 92-83 in Las Vegas, capping a week in which the Cougars also upset top-ranked Florida. Houston improved to 6-0 heading into a Nov. 27 home opener.

Source: AP via ABC News

College basketball's top four all lose in the same week, and Kansas is the new No. 1

Florida, UConn, Michigan State and Duke — the four teams that opened the week atop the Coaches Poll — all lost within two days of one another, scrambling the rankings. Kansas, at 5-0, moved into ESPN's new No. 1 spot with Houston right behind at No. 2.

Source: ESPN

WNBA

Vanessa Bryant denies joining a bid for the Sparks as the $700 million sale process heats up

With Mark Walter reportedly selling the Sparks for around $700 million, a report linked Vanessa Bryant and Pau Gasol to a potential bidding group, which Bryant publicly denied on social media. Other reported bidders include Padres owners José Feliciano and Kwanza Jones, along with a women's-sports-focused investment group.

Source: CBS Sports

Sparks' coaching search remains wide open, months after firing Lynne Roberts

Nearly two months after firing Lynne Roberts following a playoff-less two years, Los Angeles still hasn't hired a replacement; new GM Ariana Andonian says the team will "cast a wide net" for a strong developer of talent. Roneeka Hodges, Kristi Toliver and Teresa Weatherspoon have all been floated as candidates, with Toliver also linked to the Liberty's ongoing search.

Source: CBS Sports

NCAAW

Pressure keeps building on Columbia coach Megan Griffith after former players' letter

A month after serving a three-game suspension, Columbia women's basketball coach Megan Griffith faces renewed scrutiny: fifteen former players sent the university a letter asking for further action over her conduct, alongside a resurfaced 2019 allegation the school says it previously investigated and found unfounded. A university official says Griffith remains under additional oversight.

Source: Ivy Hoops Online

Iowa State suspends retiring coach Bill Fennelly one game over a sideline incident

Iowa State suspended Bill Fennelly, who is retiring at season's end, for one game after he made contact with guard Sienna Harvey's forehead while yelling at her during a huddle in a win over UIC. Associate head coach Caroline McCombs filled in while Fennelly sat out, and he has since apologized publicly.

Source: Daily Caller

MLB

Rockies take on Luis Castillo's contract in a trade with the White Sox

Colorado acquired right-handers Luis Castillo and Davis Martin from Chicago for left-hander Sean Sullivan and catching prospect Douglas Veliz. It's largely a salary dump: Castillo struggled badly after being dealt to the White Sox and is owed roughly $22-24 million for 2027, while Martin, who went 9-8 with a 3.95 ERA in 28 starts, is the more useful piece heading back to Colorado.

Source: ESPN

Tarik Skubal's free-agent market narrows as MLB's lockout deadline looms

Skubal has now met with the Mets, Dodgers, Blue Jays and Padres, with insiders still pointing to the Mets and Dodgers as favorites for a deal that could land north of $325 million. Teams are racing to get contracts like his done before Dec. 1, when the current labor deal expires and a lockout is widely expected if owners push for a salary cap the players' union has already called a non-starter.

Source: MLB.com

Horse Racing

Anti-doping unit clears every drug sample from the Breeders' Cup

The Horseracing Integrity and Welfare Unit announced that all post-race and TCO2 samples from the Oct. 30-31 Breeders' Cup at Keeneland came back clean, with no positives reported across more than 340 out-of-competition tests run in seven countries beforehand. Purse distribution for the meet has now begun.

Source: BloodHorse

Oaklawn Park sets a record stakes slate for its earliest-ever season opening

Oaklawn's 2026-27 meet opens Nov. 27 — the earliest start in the track's history — with an $18.79 million, 61-race stakes program headlined by the Hot Springs Stakes on opening day.

Source: Horse Racing Nation

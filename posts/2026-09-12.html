NFL

Patriots place A.J. Brown on IR with a high-ankle sprain

Star wide receiver A.J. Brown, acquired by New England from Philadelphia in a June trade, got his foot trapped awkwardly during Sunday's season-opening loss to Seattle, and an MRI confirmed a high-ankle sprain. The Patriots placed him on injured reserve on Friday, a move that guarantees he'll miss at least four games — including matchups against Pittsburgh, Jacksonville, Buffalo and Las Vegas — as New England tries to keep its passing game afloat without him.

Source: NFL.com

Bears lock up D'Andre Swift on a three-year extension after a career year

Chicago signed running back D'Andre Swift to a three-year, $33.75 million extension with $27.5 million guaranteed, keeping him with the Bears through 2029 instead of letting him play out the final year of his current deal. The move rewards a breakout 2025 season in which Swift piled up nearly 1,400 yards from scrimmage and 10 touchdowns, and signals Chicago sees him as a long-term piece of Ben Johnson's offense.

Source: Chicago Sun-Times

CFB

No. 23 Missouri outlasts Kansas 38-21 in a Border War shootout

Missouri quarterback Austin Simmons threw for 292 yards and three touchdowns as the Tigers held off Kansas in Friday night's renewed "Border War" rivalry game, with running back Jamal Roberts adding 125 yards and a score on the ground. A pair of second-half takeaways forced by linebacker Robert Woodyard Jr. proved decisive in keeping Kansas out of scoring range down the stretch.

Source: KSAT

Rutgers' new starter throws three touchdowns — and four picks — in a loss to Boston College

Days after being named Rutgers' starting quarterback, AJ Surace had a wildly uneven debut in a 28-21 loss at Boston College, throwing for 230 yards and three touchdowns but also four interceptions that helped keep the Eagles in front. BC quarterback Mason McKenzie outdueled him with a 78-yard touchdown pass and a rushing score in the program's annual Red Bandanna Game, dropping Rutgers to 0-2 on the season.

NBA

Suns center Mark Williams has shoulder surgery, expected out until February

Phoenix center Mark Williams underwent surgery Thursday to repair a torn labrum in his left shoulder that he suffered during an offseason workout, with a recovery timeline pointing toward a return around the All-Star break in mid-February. It's an early setback for Williams, who signed a fully guaranteed three-year, $38 million deal with the Suns this offseason after a career-best season in Phoenix's frontcourt rotation.

Source: Yahoo Sports

NBA board of governors set to tackle the Clippers' punishment, the Kawhi trade and expansion

The league's Board of Governors meets Monday and Tuesday in New York, with the Clippers' salary-cap-circumvention penalty — five forfeited first-round picks, a $30 million fine, and suspensions for owner Steve Ballmer, president Lawrence Frank and COO Gillian Zucker — topping the agenda after the team signaled it may challenge the ruling. Commissioner Adam Silver is expected to address the sanctions publicly for the first time, while governors are also expected to discuss finalizing the long-stalled Kawhi Leonard trade to Toronto and voting on expansion franchises for Seattle and Las Vegas.

Source: Hoops Rumors

NCAAM

A court order throws the NCAA's new eligibility rules into chaos

Former Purdue forward Trey Kaufman-Renn, drafted by Minnesota in June but never signed, won a temporary restraining order from an Indiana judge Wednesday blocking the NCAA from denying him a sixth year of eligibility over a season he lost to injury — and promptly entered the transfer portal. It's part of a wider legal fight over the NCAA's new age-based eligibility model, which had a separate, high-stakes hearing in a Texas case involving more than 40 athletes on Friday, leaving the rules governing who can play this season still unsettled just as roster decisions lock in.

Source: ESPN

Top-ranked 2027 prospect CJ Rosser trims his list to seven schools

CJ Rosser, ranked as high as the No. 1 overall recruit in the 2027 class, cut his list to Kentucky, North Carolina, Louisville, Arkansas, Miami, NC State and USC on Thursday and lined up an official-visit circuit running into late October, including a trip for Kentucky's Big Blue Madness. The 6-foot-10 forward is one of the sport's most sought-after long-term targets, and his eventual decision will shape several programs' rebuilding timelines.

Source: Zagsblog

WNBA

Germany and France crash into the Women's World Cup semifinals for the first time

Host Germany beat Belgium 93-74 and France knocked off China 90-61 on Thursday, sending both countries to the FIBA Women's World Cup semifinals in Berlin for the first time in their history. It sets up a loaded Final Four: Germany faces France, and a Team USA squad already through to the semifinal meets Spain in a rematch of the 2014 gold-medal game, with the winners advancing to Sunday's final.

Source: Washington Times

WNBA players start weighing in on who should replace Cathy Engelbert

Days after Cathy Engelbert announced her retirement as WNBA commissioner, ESPN reported seven names in the mix to succeed her — including Liberty CEO Keia Clarke, WNBPA president Nneka Ogwumike and NBA deputy commissioner Mark Tatum — and players have begun publicly staking out what they want from the hire. Chicago's Natasha Cloud said this week she wants the next commissioner focused on growing the game globally and investing more in officiating, while acknowledging the final call rests with the owners.

Source: ESPN

NCAAW

UConn opens on top of the first preseason women's Top 25

UConn was named the preseason No. 1 in an early women's basketball poll released this week, with UCLA, South Carolina and Texas rounding out the top four behind returning national player of the year Sarah Strong and a Huskies roster that brings back ten players. USC still cracked the rankings near the top despite reigning national Player of the Year JuJu Watkins easing back into practice from a long-term ACL injury rather than being fully cleared.

Dawn Staley's fall recruiting swing includes a marquee 2027 visit

South Carolina is hosting a wave of 2027 official visits this fall, including four-star Oregon guard Kaylor Buse, as Dawn Staley looks to add a small but targeted class in the mold of her 2023 and 2024 hauls. Buse is also weighing visits to Oregon, Washington, TCU and Utah, making the Gamecocks just one stop in a competitive recruitment.

MLB

Brewers clinch a playoff spot with the most lopsided win in a clinching game in MLB history

Milwaukee routed Cincinnati 20-0 on Friday to clinch its eighth postseason berth in nine seasons, a margin Elias Sports Bureau says is the largest ever in a clinching game. Garrett Mitchell hit a grand slam and William Contreras drove in six runs as the Brewers, who also blew out Seattle 22-0 back in August, improved to 92-56 on the year.

Source: Yahoo Sports

Twins star Byron Buxton's season ends with hip surgery

Minnesota announced Friday that center fielder Byron Buxton will have arthroscopic surgery on his right hip and miss the rest of the season, capping a year in which recurring hip trouble limited him to 93 games despite a .254/.319/.511 line with 25 home runs. Manager Derek Shelton said the team expects Buxton back for the start of next season.

Source: MLB.com

Kyle Tucker's grand slam extends the Dodgers' win streak to eight

Kyle Tucker hit his sixth career grand slam and fell a double short of the cycle in a 6-2 win over Miami on Friday night, stretching Los Angeles' winning streak to a season-high eight games as the Dodgers roll into the stretch run.

NHL

Red Wings strip Dylan Larkin of the captaincy amid a trade-request standoff

Detroit announced Friday that Dylan Larkin, captain since January 2021, has been stripped of the "C" and that the team will open the season without naming a replacement, days after Larkin requested a trade to a shortlist reportedly including Florida, Minnesota, Vegas and Dallas. The Red Wings said any decision on a new captain will wait until a permanent general manager is hired, and Larkin confirmed he's not going anywhere just yet.

Larkin said he "will be at training camp with my teammates."

Source: Local10/AP

Blue Jackets GM says he's confident a Fantilli deal gets done before camp

With restricted free agent Adam Fantilli still unsigned about a week before Columbus opens training camp, GM Don Waddell said talks with Fantilli's camp remain ongoing and the sticking point has been contract length rather than money. Waddell expressed confidence a deal closes before players report Sept. 16.

Waddell said, "I'm 100% that we'll get this thing done."

Source: NHL Rumors

UFC

Topuria's camp shuts down a Pimblett fight, presses for an immediate Gaethje rematch

Ilia Topuria's manager Malki Kawa said this week the team has no interest in a fight against Paddy Pimblett and is focused solely on landing an immediate rematch with lightweight champion Justin Gaethje, who handed Topuria his first pro loss and reportedly broke both of his orbital bones and a bone in his foot in the process. Gaethje has said he isn't interested in fighting again right away and may sit out the rest of 2026, leaving the champion's next move unresolved.

Source: Bloody Elbow

Dana White floats a Topuria-Pimblett superfight, calling Pimblett box-office gold

UFC president Dana White said this week that Paddy Pimblett remains hugely valuable to the promotion regardless of his recent loss to Gaethje, and suggested a matchup between Pimblett and Topuria could be built into a rare, McGregor-style spectacle event. Pimblett himself said he has no fight booked and hasn't been contacted about an opponent, though he'd welcome a Topuria fight if the UFC wants to make it.

White said Pimblett "gained more followers in that loss than in his last win."

Boxing

Abass Baraou upsets hometown favorite Giovani Santillan to win a vacant welterweight title

Former titleholder Abass Baraou outworked Giovani Santillan over 10 rounds in Oceanside, California on Friday, winning a majority decision to claim the vacant WBO Intercontinental welterweight title in front of Santillan's hometown crowd. Unofficial punch-stats had Baraou landing significantly more shots, including a wide edge in power punches, in a result that snaps Santillan's momentum after he'd entered as the betting favorite.

Source: Boxingnews24

Opetaia and Mikaelian meet in a cruiserweight unification bout on tonight's Garcia-Benn card

Unbeaten cruiserweight champion Jai Opetaia faces WBC titleholder Noel Mikaelian in a title-unification bout co-featured on tonight's Garcia-Benn card in Las Vegas, with both fighters framing it as the matchup that finally makes sense for the division. Mikaelian, a two-time champion, called it a true 50-50 fight he's been chasing for some time.

Opetaia called it "the fight that makes sense for boxing and for the cruiserweight division."

Source: Bad Left Hook

Golf

Europe sweeps Saturday foursomes to seize control of the Solheim Cup

Team Europe won three of four foursomes matches Saturday morning at Bernardus Golf, building on Friday's 4-4 tie to grab a 7-5 lead over the Americans, with Leona Maguire and Esther Henseleit among the pairs delivering points. The lone U.S. win came from Alison Lee and Lauren Coughlin, who beat Charley Hull and Lottie Woad, but Europe now heads into Saturday afternoon's fourball session in firm control with Sunday's singles still to come.

Horse Racing

Reigning Horse of the Year Sovereignty scratched from the Jockey Club Gold Cup

Trainer Bill Mott confirmed Friday that Sovereignty, last year's Kentucky Derby winner and the current Breeders' Cup Classic favorite, will skip the Sept. 18 Jockey Club Gold Cup at Belmont because of a foot abscess that's kept him off the track for more than a week. Mott's new target is the Oct. 31 Breeders' Cup Classic at Keeneland, echoing a similar late scratch for Sovereignty around this event a year ago.

Mott said, "We might only be 50-50 to make the race."

Source: Thoroughbred Daily News

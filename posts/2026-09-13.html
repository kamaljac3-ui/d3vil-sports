CFB

Texas erases a 20-point fourth-quarter deficit to shock No. 1 Ohio State, 24-23

Down 23-3 entering the final period, Arch Manning directed three straight scoring drives for Texas, capped by a go-ahead touchdown with 25 seconds left, in what's being called the largest fourth-quarter comeback ever against a No. 1 team. Ohio State's Julian Sayin and Jeremiah Smith put up big individual numbers, but the Buckeyes' defense couldn't close it out, dropping Ohio State to 1-1.

Source: ESPN

Oklahoma State upsets No. 6 Oregon a week after its own blowout loss

A week removed from losing to Tulsa in new coach Eric Morris' debut, Oklahoma State came in as more-than-three-touchdown underdogs and beat Oregon 39-31 behind Drew Mestemaker's 317 passing yards and a go-ahead fourth-quarter touchdown run. The win snaps a long losing streak against FBS competition for the Cowboys, a year after Oregon had routed them by more than 60 points.

Morris said postgame, "I don't know if I can top this."

Source: FootballScoop

The win kept Michigan unbeaten and handed Oklahoma its first loss of the season.

NBA

Pelicans land Bennedict Mathurin in a three-team scramble for cap room

Indiana agreed to rescind restricted free agent Bennedict Mathurin's qualifying offer, freeing him to sign a two-year, $16 million deal with New Orleans that closed September 10. To create the space, the Pelicans shipped Jordan Hawkins, Micah Peavy and draft compensation to Memphis for AJ Johnson and Taj Gibson, with Mathurin reportedly taking less money on a shorter term so he can return to unrestricted free agency again next summer.

Source: BVM Sports

Jalen Duren appears ready to bet on himself instead of signing a Pistons extension

With Detroit's training camp approaching, center Jalen Duren is reportedly leaning toward playing out next season on his $9.6 million qualifying offer rather than accept an extension worth roughly $35 million a year against his ask of $40 million-plus. Taking the qualifying offer would make Duren an unrestricted free agent next summer, giving him more leverage but stripping the Pistons of matching rights.

Source: Detroit Sports Nation

Derrick Jones Jr. isn't asking out of L.A. despite the Clippers' cap-penalty fallout

More than a week after the league fined the Clippers $30 million and stripped five first-round picks over salary-cap circumvention, reporting indicates forward Derrick Jones Jr. hasn't requested a trade and that both sides remain open to extension talks worth roughly $13 million a year. Jones is entering the final season of his current deal at about $10.5 million.

NCAAM

A federal judge deals the latest blow in college basketball's eligibility fight

A federal judge denied a preliminary injunction sought by 14 Marshall and Ohio University athletes, including basketball players, who wanted to play in 2026-27 under the NCAA's new eligibility framework, ruling the league isn't required to apply it retroactively to athletes who've already used four seasons. It's the latest split decision in a legal fight where different courts have reached opposite conclusions on nearly identical questions, leaving the picture murky for programs still finalizing 2026-27 rosters.

Source: Sportico

Minnesota's Cade Tyson is back with the team, but still can't play

Gophers forward Cade Tyson has rejoined the program and enrolled for 2026-27 even though he remains ineligible after an appeals court paused a broader injunction that had briefly restored eligibility for players from his 2022 recruiting class. Tyson is a named plaintiff in the case testing whether that class deserves a fifth season, and coach Niko Medved says a return to the court is still possible depending on how the litigation plays out.

Source: Star Tribune
